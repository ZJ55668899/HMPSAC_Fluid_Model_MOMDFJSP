ABSTRACT
In the context of Industry 4.0, manufacturers face pressure to personalize products and accelerate the supply 
chain. This requires rapid response to volatile production schedules, ensuring a balance between operational 
efficiency and product quality. Moreover, the rapid development and convergence of the cloud computing, 
Internet of Things (IoT), and big data have expanded the need for real-time tracking and adaptive scheduling to 
address uncertainties, such as equipment downtime, supply variation, and ongoing product revisions. The 
capability of IoT has significantly improved the continuous monitoring and data analysis, emphasizing the 
importance of developing effective real-time scheduling solutions in the manufacturing system. In response to 
these evolving industrial requirements, and driven by objectives to reduce the makespan, total tardiness, and 
energy consumption, we study the multi-objective multiplicity dynamic flexible job shop scheduling problem 
(MOMDFJSP), to cope with the challenges of new order arrivals and machine breakdowns in the IoT-enabled 
manufacturing system. This study proposes a novel hierarchical multi-policy deep reinforcement learning 
framework for IoT-infused manufacturing environments, aiming to integrate these diverse requirements and 
uncertainties into a coherent and responsive scheduling framework. The proposed framework comprises an 
upper-level control policy network and three lower-level objective policy networks. The upper-level and lower- 
level networks are respectively responsible for selecting temporary optimization objectives and specific dis -
patching rules. Based on the proposed framework, we design a two-stage training approach named the hierar -
chical multi-policy soft actor-critic (HMPSAC) algorithm to train multiple policy networks. In addition, we 
develop a fluid model to design the state features and dispatching rules that act as inputs and outputs, respec -
tively, for the deep reinforcement learning (DRL) policy network. The comparative analysis with well-known 
dispatching rules and DRL-based methods reveals the superior performance of HMPSAC algorithm.
1. Introduction
With the acceleration of economic globalization and the intensifi -
cation of market competition, notable changes are emerging in the 
manufacturing industry. The manufacturing paradigm has shifted from a 
manual, extensive, and passive mode to a transparent, lean, and pro -
active approach. In modern manufacturing systems, the dynamic 
interplay of machines, tools, materials, and labor leads to fluctuant 
scenarios. Emergent issues such as machine breakdowns, tool degrada -
tion, material inconsistencies, and labor fluctuations, compounded by 
unforeseen orders, underscore the need for resilient scheduling 
frameworks [1] . These frameworks must dynamically adapt to such 
variability. The inherent dynamics of manufacturing environments drive 
research into the dynamic flexible job shop scheduling problem (DFJSP), 
where the goal is to optimize the allocation of jobs to resources while 
adeptly navigating the complexities introduced by such dynamic events.
As a classical production scheduling problem, the DFJSP involves 
random job arrivals, uncertain processing times, and alterations in due 
dates. Then, DFJSP is a more complex NP-hard problem than static 
flexible job shop scheduling problem (FJSP) and job shop scheduling 
problems (JSP) [2] . In numerous manufacturing environments, the pre -
vailing operational mode is characterized by multi-variety and

small-batch production. This approach typically entails the production 
of a diverse array of products in relatively modest quantities, necessi -
tating a high degree of flexibility and efficiency in the scheduling pro -
cesses [3] . In this paper, instead of considering the division of batches, 
the multi-variety and small-batch production is represented as multi -
plicity scheduling in which each job in the batches of different job types 
is considered as an individual job and is processed separately. Although 
this approach increases the scale of the problem, it creates a customized 
schedule for every job, potentially uncovering deeper layers of optimi -
zation potential. Furthermore, with the increasingly severe environ -
mental challenges and growing emphasis on energy conservation and 
emissions reduction, reducing workshop energy consumption has 
become more important than ever. Therefore, we explore the 
multi-objective multiplicity dynamic flexible job shop scheduling 
problem, considering three objectives: total energy consumption, total 
tardiness, and maximum completion time, as well as two types of dy -
namic events: machine breakdowns and the arrival of new orders.
The MOMDFJSP is a variant of the standard DFJSP where each job 
type has multiple jobs to be processed. MOMDFJSPs can be highly 
beneficial in real-life production scenarios such as semiconductor 
manufacturing systems and motorcycle part manufacturing lines [4] . 
Addressing the MOMDFJSP can help companies improve throughput, 
reduce lead times, minimize the idle time of machines, and enhance 
resource utilization. Companies can achieve higher productivity and 
responsiveness to customer demands by effectively managing the 
scheduling of multiple job instances, resulting in improved competi -
tiveness in the market. Given its high complexity and broad applica -
bility, research on the MOMDFJSP is essential for academia and 
industry.
In addition, the rapid development of advanced information tech -
nologies, including cloud computing, the IoT, and cyber-physical sys -
tems (CPS), led to the conceptualization of the IoT-based manufacturing 
system (IoTMS). The IoTMS enables advanced management measures, 
such as timely data collection, rapid response to production distur -
bances, and accurate execution of production decisions [5,6] . Therefore, 
recent literature on real-time online scheduling in manufacturing sys -
tems emphasizes the integration of IoT and DRL for dynamic adapt -
ability [7] . However, current DRL-based scheduling multi-objective 
optimization methods encounter two main challenges. Firstly, existing 
DRL-based methods primarily use single-stage training method which 
trains multi-policy networks simultaneously, leading to issues like dis -
tribution shift, coordination problems, and a lack of diversity in Pareto 
front solutions. This method overlooks crucial factors such as variable 
objective weights and Pareto front uniformity. Secondly, most 
DRL-based methods derive state features from the problem’s charac -
teristics and select classical dispatching rules as action sets, lacking 
exploration of effective state representations and the short-sightedness 
of action rules.
To address these challenges, the data-driven hierarchical multi- 
policy deep reinforcement learning framework and two-stage training 
method are proposed. In the proposed method, we leverage the advan -
tages of fluid models in continuous system modeling to approximate the 
production process as a continuous flow of fluid, thereby assisting in the 
extraction of state features. Moreover, we utilize the asymptotic opti -
mality of fluid models to design scheduling rules, aiming to improve 
DRL’s decision-making performance and global optimization capabil -
ities. This method tracks dynamic events in real-time for immediate 
resolution of fluid models, merging these dynamic parameters with 
optimization objectives to customize state features and scheduling rules. 
These state features and scheduling rules are not only closely related to 
the discrete processing state of the workshop but also linked to the dy -
namic parameters that evolve in the fluid model. This strategy signifi -
cantly enhances the recognition and decision-making capabilities of DRL 
in workshop scheduling. The main contributions of this study include: 
(1) Different from the traditional DRL frameworks for scheduling, the 
proposed data-driven hierarchical multi-policy deep reinforce -
ment learning framework develops separate policy networks for 
different objectives.
(2) This is the first time that the fluid model is employed to solve 
multi-objective dynamic optimization problems, which extends 
the traditional state features and scheduling rules.
(3) Compared to traditional single-stage training method, the pro -
posed two-stage training method improves the stability of the 
training process.
The subsequent sections of this work are structured as follows. Sec -
tion 2 is the literature review. The problem description of the 
MOMDFJSP is discussed in Section 3 . The proposed DRL-based dynamic 
scheduling method is described in Section 4 . Section 5 provides the re -
sults and analyses. Finally, conclusions and future recommendations are 
provided in Section 6 .
2. Literature review
Due to the scarcity of literature on DFJSP with multiplicity charac -
teristics, we reviewed the literature on classical DFJSP. DFJSPs are a 
common problem in practical industrial systems. Researchers have 
proposed various optimization approaches to solve DFJSP, mainly 
including metaheuristic-based and deep reinforcement learning-based 
methods. Because the complexity and real-time requirements of DFJSP 
make it difficult for exact and heuristic algorithms to meet these intri -
cate requirements and obtain acceptable solutions [8] . Moreover, 
considering the lack of literature on using fluid models to solve the FJSP, 
we analyze the application of fluid models in JSP to unveil its potential 
application value in solving the FJSP.
2.1. Metaheuristic methods for DFJSP
Numerous metaheuristic algorithms have been proposed for solving 
DFJSP. Gholami and Zandieh [9] introduced a simulated genetic algo -
rithm adept at handling simulated random failures. Through the inte -
gration of right-shift scheduling and rescheduling techniques, they 
achieved optimal solutions, establishing this method as a crucial 
approach in subsequent research. Researchers have enhanced genetic 
algorithms, including improvements in initialization and search strate -
gies [10] . An upgraded version of NSGA-II has been proposed for 
single-machine failure problems [11] , considering factors like machine 
production status, failure probability, and floating-point time for ma -
chine failures. Other improved metaheuristic algorithms have also been 
employed for single-machine failure issues [12] . For instance, Wei, et al. 
[13] proposed an enhanced cuckoo migration algorithm, while Buddala 
and Mahapatra [14] designed a teacher-learning optimization algo -
rithm. Teymourifar, et al. [15] used gene expression programming rules 
to extract scheduling rules. Ghaleb, et al. [16] considered real-time joint 
optimization of maintenance and production plans in intelligent 
manufacturing systems, addressing dynamic events such as new job 
arrivals, changes in due dates, machine degradation, and failures in 
dynamic FJSP. Lv, et al. [17] combined rescheduling with process 
planning for energy-efficient production and process flexibility, trans -
forming the problem into a mixed-integer programming model. 
Although these works successfully solve the DFJSP, the performance of 
metaheuristic methods is significantly affected by the computation time. 
Therefore, most literature overlooks the impact of rescheduling time. In 
real-time online scheduling, the rescheduling time is very short, and 
metaheuristic methods can’t achieve efficient responses in such a short 
time [8] .

patching rules and metaheuristics, DRL-based methods offer the 
advantage of adapting the most appropriate actions at each rescheduling 
point. Scheduling methods rooted in DRL exhibit notable advantages 
over conventional rule-based or heuristic approaches. They demonstrate 
heightened efficacy in reducing production costs, enhancing processing 
efficiency, and showcasing superior flexibility and generalization ca -
pabilities [18] . These features make DRL particularly well-suited for 
multi-objective optimization and real-time dynamic scheduling in 
DFJSPs. To cope with these requirements, several studies have inte -
grated DRL algorithms with deep deterministic policy gradient (DDPG) 
[19] , proximal policy optimization (PPO) [20] , Q-learning [21] , deep Q

Amidst the escalating uncertainty and complexity in manufacturing, 
re-scheduling methods to manage unexpected events have become a 
critical concern for real-time interrupt management. DRL is pivotal in 
adapting to dynamic environments, ensuring robustness without 
compromising cost-effectiveness, product quality, and delivery effi -
ciency. Zhao, et al. [24] proposed a dual-layer Q-learning algorithm for 
the DFJSP with machine failures. Baer, et al. [25] introduced a DRL 
method for online scheduling in Flexible Manufacturing Systems (FMS). 
Luo [26] designed a network framework using the DDQN to solve the 
DFJSP with new job arrivals. Palombarini and Martínez [27] presented a 
DRL method leveraging high-dimensional sensory input for efficient 
online re-scheduling in dynamic production environments. Yang and Xu 
[28] pioneered intelligent scheduling and reconfiguration studies in 
reconfigurable assembly lines with new job arrivals, employing the 
actor-critic deep reinforcement learning method with excellent perfor -
mance. Chang, et al. [29] proposed a DDQN-based architecture for deep 
reinforcement learning to solve the DFJSP with random job arrivals. Liu, 
et al. [30] introduced a hierarchical and distributed architecture, 
training agents through the DDQN to capture the relationship between 
production information and scheduling objectives for real-time de -
cisions in flexible job shop environments. Gui, et al. [31] presented a 
DDPG-based scheduling method for the DFJSP, designing a composite 
action of SDRs and continuous weight variables for a continuous rule 
space and SDR weight selection. Zhang, et al. [32] investigated a novel 
DFJSP model considering machine job processing time uncertainty, 
proposing a PPO-based deep reinforcement learning framework to 
address the issue. However, these studies employ DRL-based methods 
for the DFJSP that predominantly features single-objective optimization 
with single-layered network frameworks.
In multi-objective DFJSP literature, predominant dynamic events 
involve machine failures and random job arrivals, focusing on opti -
mizing total tardiness time and maximum completion time. Lang, et al. 
[33] introduced a deep reinforcement learning algorithm with two DQN 
agents handling operation selection and machine allocation. Luo, et al. 
[34] devised a hierarchical multi-agent deep reinforcement learning 
framework, utilizing a multi-agent PPO training algorithm for partial 
no-wait multi-objective DFJSP. Furthermore, Luo, et al. [35] proposed 
an online rescheduling framework based on dual-layer DDQN for 
multi-objective DFJSP with random job arrivals. Li, et al. [36] addressed 
the DFJSP considering insufficient transportation resources, optimizing 
for maximum completion time and total energy consumption, and pre -
senting a hybrid DQN solution for dynamic interference events. Wang, 
et al. [37] introduced a real-time processing framework using two DQN 
networks for online scheduling of multi-objective DFJSP, achieving 
notable optimization rates. Finally, Wu, et al. [38] explored DFJSP 
under random job arrivals, proposing a dual-layer DDQN network 
combining DRL with scheduling rules, optimizing for makespan and 
total tardiness time. Zhang, et al. [39] presented an adaptive multi-task 
multi-objective scheduling model using a hybrid strategy and a 
two-hierarchy deep reinforcement learning architecture to optimize 
total manufacturing time and cost in dynamic environments. These 
prevailing literature on multi-objective DFJSP with DRL focuses on 
addressing machine failures and random job arrivals, targeting the 
optimization of tardiness and completion times.
In addition, it is evident that hierarchical DRL is the main approach 
for tackling multi-objective DFJSPs. Initially proposed by Kulkarni, et al. 
[40] , hierarchical DRL integrates value functions over different time 
scales with intrinsically motivated learning to improve exploration ef -
ficiency in sparse feedback environments. Subsequently, various hier -
archical DRL methods have been developed for multi-objective 
optimization [41] . These methods typically feature a hierarchical 
network structure, which can be categorized into two types: the first 
involves an upper-level network responsible for selecting intermediate 
optimization objectives, while the lower-level network consists of 
operation and machine policy networks for operation selection and 
machine assignment [33,34] . The second type uses the lower-level 
network as a composite scheduling rule selection network, specifically 
for operation selection and machine allocation [35–39] . Our approach 
differs by using separate policy networks for different objectives. In 
addition, traditional frameworks often use a single-stage training 
approach, which can destabilize training due to synchronous optimiza -
tion of the two-layer network. Therefore, we propose a two-stage 
training method to enhance stability.

tion when dealing with high-multiplicity JSPs. However, their effec -

rendering them unsuitable for workshop scheduling in low-multiplicity 
production scenarios. In addition, compared to JSP, the FJSP is more 
complex, involving not only the sequencing of operations but also the 
allocation of machines, which makes it more difficult and challenging to 
develop fluid models and their solution strategies for the FJSP. There -
fore, this paper develops the fluid model specifically for the FJSP that 
leverages their global optimization capabilities. The proposed integra -
tion of fluid models with deep reinforcement learning is suitable for 
solving FJSPs ranging from low to high multiplicity, thereby enhancing 
the practical application potential of fluid models in actual production.
3. Problem description
The MOMDFJSP consists of M machines ( m = 1 , 2 , ..., M ), S orders 
with random arrival times ( s = { 1 , 2 , … , S } ), and R job types ( r =
{ 1 , 2 , ..., R } ), where job type r consists of J
r 
operations. All jobs are 
categorized into R distinct types. In the same job, operations follow a 
specified sequence of precedence. An operation may be executed on 
multiple machines, with varying processing times and cutting powers 
across different machines. It is important to note that each machine is 
characterized by two attributes: processing power and idle power, with 
the latter being a constant value. In the event of a machine malfunction, 
it ceases to consume energy.
The orders s arrive randomly, containing N

an actual manufacturing environment, the arrival of new orders con -
taining multiple jobs for each job type is assumed to follow a Poisson 
distribution [51] , which implies that the time interval between the ar -
rivals of new orders follows an exponential distribution [52] . Each 
machine m might break down randomly. The start time of the k th 
breakdown on machine m is represented as bs

. To manage the 
MOMDFJSP ’ s complexity, several assumptions are employed in 
problem-solving approaches: 
(1) All machines are limited to processing only one operation at any 
given time.
(2) Operations are non-preemptive, meaning they can ’ t be 
interrupted.
(3) Operations follow a strict order, and one can ’ t begin until the 
previous operation is performed.
(4) Setup and transportation time are not considered.
(5) If a machine breakdown interrupts an operation, it should be 
resumed immediately on the same machine after repair.

the fluid model accurately reflects instantaneous changes in the pro -

manufacturing system, state features and scheduling rules that interact 
with the dynamic parameters of the fluid model are designed to enhance 
state representation capability and the quality of decision-making ac -

continuous system state information provided by the fluid model as 
input for learning and decision-making. This not only promotes precise 
characterization of the manufacturing process, but also enhances the 
adaptability and optimization performance of the scheduling rules. 
Consequently, the proposed framework fully leverages the advantages of 
fluid models in describing system dynamics and harnessing the capa -
bilities of deep reinforcement learning for decision optimization, 
thereby aiming to enhance the performance of scheduling systems.

4.1.2. Soft actor-critic algorithm
The SAC algorithm is an entropy-based maximization method [53] . 
By incorporating entropy into the objective function, the SAC signifi -
cantly enhances its exploration capabilities and robustness, striving to 
achieve the balance between reward and entropy (i.e., the randomness 
of the policy). The algorithm encourages higher rewards for actions 
executed with greater randomness, thereby averting premature 
convergence to suboptimal policy. A higher entropy value leads to more 
exploration, thus avoiding policy convergence to local optima. 
Furthermore, the multi-objective optimization problems typically 
involve complex decision and objective spaces. The maximum entropy 
characteristic of SAC can maintain the balance between multiple ob -
jectives. The optimal policy of SAC is represented as Eq. (16) . In Eq. (16) ,

4.8. Hierarchical multi-policy network framework
The proposed hierarchical multi-policy network framework is shown 
in Fig. 3 , which integrates an upper-level control policy network and 
three lower-level objective policy networks. The upper-level control 
policy network is used for selecting a lower-level objective policy 
network according to action a

are identical and separate networks that share the same network 
structure. Each objective policy network consists an operation policy 
network and a machine policy network, which are used to select oper -
ation selection rules and machine assignment rules, respectively.
The control policy network and the three objective policy networks 
share the same state features, as defined in Section 4.4 . The control 
policy network utilizes the three objective policy networks as its actions. 
Each objective policy network executes action selection based on 
defined operational rules and machine assignment rules. Furthermore, 
the three objective policy networks are assigned distinct reward func -
tions: r

these reward functions are provided in Section 4.7 .

4.9. Two-stage training method
The two-stage training methodology involves the training of objec -
tive policy networks and control policy networks, as depicted in Fig. 6 . 
Initially, each objective policy network is trained separately based on 
state features and scheduling rules using the Advantage Actor-Critic 
(A2C) algorithm. Each objective policy network is guided by its corre -
sponding reward function, which ensures that the network converges 
effectively within the context of its specific objective. Upon the 
completion of this stage, the trained objective policy networks are used 
as potential actions for the subsequent training of the control policy 
network using the SAC algorithm. Decision points t, are defined as the 
moments of new order arrivals, machine breakdowns, and the comple -
tion of processing tasks by machines. The detailed algorithms for both 
the objective policy network training and control policy network 
training stages are delineated as follows.

5.1. Training process
During training, a new instance is created at random in each training 
epoch to increase the generalizability of the proposed HMPSAC algo -
rithm in various production scenarios. The algorithm parameters used in 
the training process are shown in Table 7 . In each training epoch, a 
newly generated training instance is used as the environment to train the 
policy network. The training environment, developed using Python, is 
available at https://github.com/Linshan-Ding/HMPSAC_Fluid_Mode 
l_MOMDFJSP/tree/main/environments . After each training epoch, the 
HMPSAC algorithm is tested on a generated validation instance, where 
the problem parameters M, S, and DDT are set to 15, 3, and 1.0, 
respectively. Fig. 7 shows that the three objective values of the test 
instance for each training epoch are based on the validation instance. 
The training curve indicates that the three targeted values decrease 
progressively throughout the training process and begin to stabilize after 
400 cycles. This suggests that the HMPSAC algorithm is capable of 
effectively learning applicable scheduling strategies through its inter -
action with the shop scheduling environment.

5.4. Comparison with DRL-based algorithms
To further validate the superiority of the proposed hierarchical

comparative analysis was conducted against four other multi-objective 
optimization methods based on deep reinforcement learning. These 
methods include the Hybrid Double Deep Q-Network (HDQN) [26] , 
Dynamic Multi-objective Double Deep Q-Network (DMDDQN) [37] , 
Two-Hierarchy Deep Q-Network (THDQN) [35] , and Hierarchy 
Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization (HMAPPO) [34] . Our eval -
uation employed generational distance (GD) [61] , inverse generational 
distance (IGD) [55] , and Spread [62] as metrics. GD measures the 
average distance between solutions in an algorithm ’ s Pareto front and 
the nearest solutions in the true Pareto front, with lower values indi -
cating better performance in terms of proximity to the true front. Spread 
quantifies how well an algorithm explores the entire Pareto front, with 
lower values indicating a more diverse approximation of the front.
For each instance, each method is independently run 30 times. 
Table 12 presents the outcomes of the experiments. The results indicate 
that the HMPSAC framework, which uses a hierarchical multi-policy 
network structure, outperforms other deep reinforcement learning 
methods such as HDQN, THDQN, HMAPPO, and DMDDQN in terms of 
the Generational Distance (GD), Inverted Generational Distance (IGD), 
and Spread metrics. In addition, HMPSAC also achieved better overall 
average values for GD, IGD, and Spread across 27 instances compared to 
the other four deep reinforcement learning methods, further confirming 
the necessity and effectiveness of implementing a hierarchical multi- 
policy network structure.

metrics of the HMPSAC and other comparative algorithms across all

consistently achieves significantly smaller values in the GD, IGD, and 
Spread metrics for instances of varying sizes. Furthermore, the data 
points for GD and IGD are densely clustered around smaller values, 
indicating that HMPSAC performs stably when solving MOMDFJSP 
problems of different scales.
To visually demonstrate the performance of all algorithms in solving 
the MOMDFJSP, Fig. 12 displays the Pareto frontiers obtained by all

convergence for problems of different sizes. This indicates that the 
HMPSAC algorithm excels in addressing problems of varying scales and 
complexities, exhibiting strong universality and adaptability.

6. Conclusion
This paper presents a novel approach for the multi-objective multi -
plicity dynamic flexible job-shop scheduling problem with machine 
failures and new order arrivals, aiming to optimize the maximum 
completion time, total tardiness, and total energy consumption. A data- 
driven hierarchical multi-policy network framework is established, 
comprising a control policy network and three objective policy net -
works. To balance multiple objectives and obtain high-quality Pareto 
solutions, the proposed framework selects an objective policy network at

decision points based on temporary targets identified by the control 
network. To further enhance this framework, state features, operation 
selection rules, and machine assignment rules are developed by inte -
grating the fluid model with objective characteristics. Furthermore, a 
composite reward function is crafted to steer the algorithm towards the 
global Pareto front. The state feature and scheduling rule formulated in 
this research improves conventional static rule-based dispatching 
methodologies by combining real time processing states under the 
workshop environment with dynamic parameters derived from fluid 
models. This novel approach allows for real-time tracking of dynamic 
events, enabling quick resolution in fluid model scenarios. Integrating 
these dynamic parameters with the optimization objectives refines the 
customization of state features and scheduling rules. This innovative 
strategy significantly improves the analytical and decision-making per -
formance of DRL when applied to workshop scheduling scenarios. In 
addition, a two-stage training methodology incorporating maximum 
entropy reinforcement learning is proposed to improve training stability 
and enrich the diversity of Pareto solutions. The effectiveness of our 
approach has been validated through comparisons with well-known 
dispatching rules and other multi-objective dynamic scheduling algo -
rithms. The results demonstrate the capability of our method to make 
efficient real-time decisions and generate a high-quality Pareto front. 
Our research provides a robust framework for addressing the complex -
ities of modern manufacturing systems. In the future, we can integrate 
the fluid model and other network models (such as graph neural 
network and large language model) to address more complex constraints 
in the manufacturing systems.
